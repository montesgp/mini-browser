See it in action

Click the tour, then press Space to pause or R to restart. Open it full screen.

Just the page

No toolbar, no address bar, no title bar: the website sits inside a thin dark frame, so what you share is only what matters.

Tabs and windows when you reach for them

Move the mouse to the top of the window and a thin bar slides in with your tabs and window buttons. It hides again when the mouse leaves, or stays if you pin it. A tab can move to a window of its own and back.

Separate and secure

Its own cookies and history, so nothing from your everyday browser shows up in a meeting. Real Chromium with its sandbox on, and no telemetry, analytics or accounts.

Opening it the first time

Your system may warn you the first time, because the app is not code-signed yet. Here is what to do.

Windows
  1. Right-click the downloaded .zip and choose Extract All.
  2. Open the extracted folder and double-click minibrowser.exe.
  3. If you see “Windows protected your PC”, click More info, then Run anyway.

To skip that warning: before extracting, right-click the .zip → Properties → tick Unblock → OK.

macOS (Apple Silicon)
  1. Double-click the downloaded .zip and move minibrowser.app to Applications.
  2. The first time, right-click (or Control-click) the app and choose Open.
  3. If it still will not open, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway.

It runs on Macs with Apple Silicon (M1 or newer); there is no build for Intel Macs yet.

Linux (x64)
  1. Extract the downloaded .tar.gz (or run tar -xzf mini-browser-linux-x64.tar.gz).
  2. In the extracted folder, run ./minibrowser.
  3. If it stops with a sandbox error, follow the Linux sandbox setup.

Keep the extracted folder together: the app needs the files next to it.
